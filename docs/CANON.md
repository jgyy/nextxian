The facts the story keeps, so that a scene written in Book Five agrees with one written in Book One. When two scenes disagree, this page decides. The `docs/PLOT_HOLES.md` ledger lists the contradictions that were fixed against it.

## Facts that must not move

- **The sealed.** Fifteen were sealed. Fourteen are on the Law Hall’s ledger, signed by Wanru. The fourteenth is a girl of fifteen who says she was never dead, only listening. The fifteenth is Su Mengjiao, sealed by the masters’ seal alone.
- **The Elder.** Su Wanru is 43. She was Registrar on the Sealing Night and has been Elder for twelve years. She is afraid of heights. She keeps two ledgers: the Law’s sentences, and what she felt when she signed.
- **Mengjiao.** Four years younger than Wanru. Braid, silver comb, a wordless tune. She believes the abyss is grieving, not hungry. The price of the Blood Lotus is not a name but the listener: whoever hears must keep listening.
- **The Sealing Jade.** Whole on the Sealing Night. The masters split it: the upper half stayed in the altar, the lower half went down the path with Mengjiao, and a small shard was kept in the vault under the Ash Archive. The jade is a piece of the **Ledger Stone**, on which Heaven’s Ledger is copied.
- **Mortals.** A Mortal has no gathered qi but can draw a thread of raw breath-qi, enough to put out a lantern or read a mark. Qi Condensation is the first gathered qi.
- **The Wanderer** carries a cold black seed in the chest, from childhood. It lets them live in the mist. They were raised by a teacher in a river village who told them nothing, and they have no name they will say.
- **Ren Qiao** is 32. He held the lantern for the fourteen when he was 17, and was taught qi by Mengjiao in the outer courtyard.
- **Gu Hanzhen** is 19. His brother Gu Hanming was taken by the levy in the year of the Sealing, when Hanzhen was four. His mother Gu Yunxia keeps a tea stall in the foothill village.
- **Lin Yuan** is 17, the child his mother was expelled for. He keeps the keys of the Ash Archive.

## The world

**Heaven’s Ledger** is where every cultivator who rises is written. To ascend, you must be written. A name that is written cannot be unmade; a name that is struck out falls to the **Unwritten**, a place beneath the world reached through *wounds* where a page was torn. The Crimson Abyss is such a wound.

**The Blood Lotus Sutra** is the art of listening to the Unwritten. It was never a weapon. The **black lotus** is a seed that lets its bearer survive the Unwritten and carry names back out of it. The founders feared it, and the Law is what they built to forbid it.

**The Concord of Nine Peaks** keeps the sects in order. Its **Censors** audit Names. **The Hollow Lotus** is a cult of the struck-out who want to be written back, or to unwrite Heaven. **Heaven’s Clerk** is whatever writes.

## The books

Each book after the first is a region and a hub. The player chooses which arcs to walk, and the climax of the book opens once enough of them are done. An arc is about forty scenes: an entry with four approaches, a hidden route, and a close that reads what the player did.

1. **The Crimson Abyss** (Book One): the Azure Cloud Sect.
2. **The River of Ledgers**: the Censors, the Concord, Qinglian Ford. The Elder is summoned, and the Reserve is named.
3. **The Drowned Library**: the Salt Coast. Where struck names wash up. The Concord decides to seal the abyss by sacrifice.
4. **The Ash Steppe**: the Listeners, the Hollow Lotus, the first Listener’s tomb. What the founders did.
5. **The Glass Sea and the Heavenly Stair**: ascension, and what the Ledger costs. The Stair is climbed by realm (the Nine Tiers), the Hall of the Ninth Night ends the Ceremony, and a lift carries the company down to the foothills.
6. **The Last Sealing**: the return to Azure Cloud. Four arcs: the Ash Bridge (Captain Dur, Ren Qiao's post, Old Lin's path), the Ash Archive (Lin Yuan, forty days behind inward wards, the eight keys and the ninth), the Crimson Abyss (nine doors, one per verb) and the Last Sealing (Su Mengjiao, who asks the abyss's one question: *who is listening?*).

### The realm ladder in canon

Mortal, Qi Condensation, Foundation Establishment, Core Formation, Nascent Soul, Spirit Severing, Dao Seeking, Ascendant. The ward on the Ash Bridge bows only to Spirit Severing (realm 5) and above; the doors of the abyss read only Dao Seeking (realm 6); no sect has had an Ascendant (realm 7) for two hundred years. A cultivator who tries a deed beyond their realm pays for it (see strain in ARCHITECTURE.md).

### Tribulations in canon

Every realm ends in a trial the sky sets before the next one opens, and every cultivator who has risen has sat all of those below them. Mortal: the **cold thread**, felt in a night of fasting, which the qi stays for. Qi Condensation: the **wayward breath**, which runs backward through the meridians once. Foundation Establishment: the **nine bells**, nine strokes of heavenly thunder. Core Formation: the **soul-fire**, which burns what the core has hidden. Nascent Soul: the **severing gale**, which cuts every cord to the dust of old names. Spirit Severing: the **Ledger’s audit**, in which whatever writes the Ledger reads a sage’s page aloud. Dao Seeking: the **nine gates**, each asking one thing and taking it. Ascendant: the **silence after**, in which the sky sends nothing, and which no sect has had a living witness of for two hundred years. A keeper may run a *rehearsal* of any of them; the sky does not rehearse. The sect bows by realm and the bow rises with it: a mortal is not looked at, a Condenser is nodded to, a Core is seated, a Dao Seeker is received, an Ascendant is kneeled to, and nobody in the sect breaks that order by accident. The Elder, Su Wanru, left the mountain fifteen years ago at twenty-eight; the Ash Bridge ward has never bowed to her.

### Dress in canon

What a person wears is read before they are spoken to, and the mountain keeps eight crafts of it, one for each realm. Mortal: **hemp**, beaten and turned on the washing-stair below the Eastern Peak, the dress of mourners and of those nobody dresses. Qi Condensation: **oilcloth**, proofed in the cloak-sheds where the Sutra Road leaves the mountain, and sold by weight against a pebble said to be from the sea. Foundation Establishment: **ink-dyed cotton**, sized in the dye-house by the tribunal river, whose water runs three colours before the bend. Core Formation: **embroidered silk**, stitched in the Salt Works in a single breath, with one stitch left loose so that heaven will not be jealous. Nascent Soul: **lacquered scale**, laced in the yard at the end of the Steppe road, where the dead are relaced by the thread of the shirts they died in. Spirit Severing: **robes of office**, fitted in the robing hall behind the Weighing, whose lining is red and is never to be seen. Dao Seeking: the **pilgrim’s dust-cloth**, which is never washed, in the hostel on the road to the Hall of the Ninth Night. Ascendant: the **Seamless Robe**, woven on a loom with no shuttle in the white valley beneath the Bridge, worn once, and not by anyone who has asked for it.

A hero begins in one of five dresses (patched hemp, an oiled cloak, an ink-stained robe, embroidered silk, a scale jerkin) and may come to own all five. The sect reads whichever the hero has on: the standing it gives, what a keeper prices, what a warden rules, what a masker lends and what a mender finds torn. A hero’s change of dress is something the Elder, Su Wanru, notices before the hero has said a word, and she says so a few decisions later. The keepsakes of a hero’s own story (the Sisters’ Book, the Wardens’ Iron Tally, the message fifteen years late, the Master’s Last Pill) are not goods, and no buyer is offered them.

## The world (fixed in the World Roads)

The mountain is not the world. Below and around it lie **ten reaches**, each a place the story already passes and each with **four affairs** going on that the Concord counts as one line in a report: the terraces below the mountain, the Pilgrim Stairs, the quarry country, the neighbour sects of the Cloud Gap, the river provinces, the Salt Coast, the Ash Steppe, the shore and camps of the Glass Sea, the Nine Tiers, and the foot of the Shut Sect. That is **forty affairs** (the Lower Granary, the Spring Levy, the Bitter Well, the Borrowed Peak, the Audit of Heron County, the Brine Quarrel, the Remount Levy, the Cage of the Descent, the Masters’ Quarrel, the Fourteen Names, and thirty more).

- **Fate.** Every affair has a fate, what it does if nobody touches it: it **mends**, it **grinds on**, or it **breaks**. Time passes in the affair whether or not the hero visits.
- **Part.** The hero may take no part (they only hear), or a small one (**look**, **hand**), or a large one (**side**, **hinge**). A look moves the affair a little; a hinge may change its course. A hero who takes only small parts across forty affairs still matters, and is told so.
- **Method.** An affair is turned by finding its lever (a flag from elsewhere in the story), forced by realm, bargained, or paid for.
- **Course.** The result is that the affair is mended, ground on, or broken, and is remembered by the people in it. The Elder hears of each (she has an opinion on the neighbour sects and on the terraces; she was Registrar for six years and has been Elder for twelve).
- **The Gathering.** Before the Last Sealing the world comes up the road. Each reach sends a voice that speaks for its four affairs, touched or not, and a reach with two voices or more **answers**. A hero who touched nothing is met by the world’s silence, and the Gathering says so.
- **Roles.** From the sum, the hero is **unseen**, a **witness**, a **hand**, one of **many** hands, or a **hinge**.

Voices in the world are not all new: Qiu (a young woman who keeps the dark Salt Lamp and writes on slates), Lian Sheng (a woman), the matron Ye Qing of the Orphans’ Terrace, Carver Sun of the Wall of Names, and the cage on glass cables that carries the descent keep the roles the earlier books gave them.

## Conventions for writers

- Scene ids use the arc’s prefix: `censors_entry`, `censors_a1s3`. Flags use the same prefix. A flag nothing reads is a defect; `npm run audit` fails on it.
- Every scene has four or more choices, a heading, and 200 words or fewer of body text. Alternatives in a group count once.
- A companion appears with `bond:id` effects. A choice that changes a bond should be one the companion would notice.
- Do not state a secret before the scene that reveals it.
- Do not use a bare name before the character is introduced.

## Book Four: The Ash Steppe

Eighteen days of grass between the coast and the mountain. The **Steppe** is silver feather-grass (“ash-grass”), kurgans, standing stones that hum, and a wind that carries names. Its people are the **Listeners**, the last of the Order of the Torn Page, who keep Yu Ling’s nine verbs as clan customs and cannot be lied to, because the wind tells them. Clans: the **Stone-Singers** (the Singing Stones), the **Glass-Wrights** (the Glass Orchard), the **Horse-Keepers** (the Winter Camp), the **Salt-Road caravaneers** (Yue Zhaoning’s kin), and the **Kurultai** of nine clans under **Grandmother Tamar**, blind, ninety, laughing, who decides. Madam Ash carries an eleven-year-old letter “to the woman who will decide”. Ren Qiao’s mother was a Horse-Keeper. Gu’s brother Hanming, taken by the levy fifteen years ago, is a sergeant in the Steppe Garrison under **Colonel Ruo**, who runs a silencing campaign (Hush bells) against the clans. Su Mengjiao is counted by the Order as its **Seventh Voice**; the player is the Ninth. Duan Wuyi joins the party at the Grass Line.

## The hero

The player makes a character before the first scene, and every part of it is something an NPC can see. None of it changes what the hero can do. Cultivation does that.

- **Name.** The name the hero gives at the gate. It replaces every “Wanderer” the story says, so nobody calls a named hero by a title. The canon still holds: the Wanderer has *no name they will say*, and the name given at the gate is the one they give strangers. The default, unchanged, is Wanderer.
- **Papers.** Outcast, commoner, gentry or noble describe the register the hero’s teacher entered them under in the river village. They are what the world believes, and they do not contradict the foundling’s truth: a teacher can enter a child under any house’s name. NPCs say “your papers”, never “your family”. The Reserve child’s real origin is revealed in Book II exactly as before.
- **Background.** The life the hero led *since* the river village: port orphan, copyist’s apprentice, levy deserter, trader’s runner, paddy hand, herbalist’s helper. It never replaces the river village or the teacher.
- **Dress, looks, gender.** What anyone sees at a glance. Gender is a woman, a man or unspoken, and the story uses `{they}`, `{them}`, `{their}`, `{honorific}` and `{person}` for the hero, so no line may assume one. Every hero carries a plain blade (the story says “blade”, never “sword”), and the story never describes the hero’s hair or face beyond what looks the player chose.
- **Standing.** How far the world bows. It is the rank of the papers (0 to 3), plus one for silk or armour and minus one for rags, plus **two for every realm**. A realm outranks any birth: a Foundation Builder born an outcast stands above a mortal prince.

## Realm roads (Books Two to Six)

Ten more waysides, one from each hub of the later books, each with four roads of 25 scenes. They are the places the road passes without naming: tollhouses, laundries, signal towers, wells. Each asks a realm that follows the pace of the story (Foundation Establishment on the river, Core Formation at Qinglian and on the Salt Coast, Nascent Soul on the Steppe, Spirit Severing at the edge of the grass, on the Glass Sea and at the tents, Dao Seeking on the Stair and on the road home).

- The **Glass Sea is glass**, fused sand with no water: nothing there has a tide, a shore to wade from, or a diver. Cutters saw mirrors from its plates.
- The Elder travels with the player in Books Two to Six, so the roads report to her in the cart or the camp, never in her hall.
- **Keepers’ names are unique.** No keeper shares a given name with anyone else in the story, and none shares a companion’s name. A test fails if one does.
- The roads never say “the Law Hall” or “the sect gate” except on the road home, where the mountain is in sight.

## Money, goods and holdings (the Fortune Roads)

- **Spirit stones** are the coin of the whole story, on the mountain and off it. A paddy hand starts with eight, a noble with sixty or more, a trader’s runner twelve more than anyone else of their birth.
- **The sect pays a stipend** to anyone living on its mountain: a stone, and one more for every realm, every eighth decision. It stops the morning the barge leaves the river steps (`departure_dawn`). After that the hero pays for their own keep. A Mortal and a Qi Condenser live on rice. From Foundation Establishment on, a cultivator eats stones: one a payday at Foundation and Core, two at Nascent Soul and Spirit Severing, three above. A cultivator who cannot pay is **Unfed**, and cultivates a fifth slower until they can.
- **Goods** are carried and counted. A tool, a relic, a scripture, a material or a token quickens cultivation by 2 to 7 percentage points once the hero has reached the realm below the road’s that sold it. A **pill** adds no speed. It carries a hero who is within 15% of the next realm’s points over the threshold, once, and is eaten doing it. Each of the ten waysides sells exactly one pill, for the realm after its own.
- **Holdings** are leases and shares. They pay on every payday and cost on every payday, add 2 to 5 percentage points of speed once the hero has the realm, and are taken back if the upkeep cannot be met. A lease costs four times the road’s base price, or two and a half times it to a hero who bought a share of the books first.
- **Three goods stand in for a realm** at a Book Six ward: the Returner’s Seven-Star Charm for the Ash Bridge (a Nascent Soul crosses), the Retired Office Seal for the Archive’s ring, and the First-Plate Shard for the abyss doors (a Spirit Severing cultivator reads them). Each is used up by the choice that spends it.
- **The ten waysides** open from `mission`, `return_hall`, `archive_dawn_2`, `jade_eye_bell_2`, `hub_departure`, `hub_asm_witnesses`, `b3_gate`, `b4_gate`, `b5_gate` and `b6_gate`, and ask Qi Condensation, Qi Condensation, Foundation Establishment (three times), Core Formation (twice), Nascent Soul, Spirit Severing and Dao Seeking. The first five are on the mountain and speak in its words; the last five are on the road and never say “the Law Hall” or “the sect gate”.
- **Keepers’ names are unique** among all 120 road keepers and the cast. A good or a holding is named without an article, since the text adds one.
- **The hero’s dress** is what they chose. “Your boots” is “your sandals” to someone in patched hemp, and “your hat” is a headcloth, a cap or a hood for everyone but the traveller. The story never gives the hero a cloak more specific than “your cloak”.
- **The Glass Sea has no water.** The shared wording of the roads says no rain, no pond and no river, so it can be used there.

## Beginnings and the Cultivation Roads

- **Twelve beginnings.** The six older starts and six that start in cultivation: the sealed cave (Qi Condensation, 22 points), the pill-house apprentice (a half-cooked pill), the roadside lamp-keeper, the hedge-listener’s ward, the one struck by heaven (Qi Condensation, 16 points) and the dreamer of a stranger’s breath. All are lives lived since the river village, so the foundling and the black seed stand. Each beginning’s speed bonus lasts while its art does (the dreamer’s ends at Spirit Severing).
- **A method.** Every new beginning ends its fourth scene on one of four methods, each a flag (`og_cave_m_settle`…). The Elder at the door and the keepers of the Cultivation Roads read it.
- **The Hush** is nineteen years old. Its bell tolls at noon in the Garrison’s gibbets and the wind goes deaf until dusk. The Listeners’ shelters teach cultivators to work through it.
- **The Cultivation Roads** open from the Ash Archive roof, the eastern peak’s lesson, the jade mirror, the sparring court, Qinglian Ford, and the Night of Stillness at each later hub, and ask Qi Condensation to Dao Seeking. The Glass Sea’s plates have no water. The Great Flood at Tidewall was forty years ago, and ninety-one drowned.

## The Crossing Roads and the gendered paths

- **Forty crossings** of five elements and eight crafts, ten waysides of one element each (the making crafts first, the walking ones after). They open from `archive_clerk`, `archive_vault`, `sutra_gu_cell`, `jade_eye_tablets`, `tribunal_testimony`, the Qinglian Ford night, `b3_close3`, the Steppe night, the Glass Sea camp and `depths_close`.
- **The Glass Sea has no water**: the Bright Rim roads say rim, plain and glare, never shore or tide. The roads on the road never say the Law Hall or the sect gate.
- **The memorial** is a hall behind the Law Hall; the Hall of Bells stands behind it. The Great Flood at Tidewall was forty years ago, and the hounds of the salt hearths would not go down to the harbour that morning.
- **Gender.** The woman’s Left Cloister kept the Sisters’ Book of fourteen lullabies; Wanru kept its keys at twenty and sang her name last. The man’s Right Yard hangs fourteen lanterns and swears the wardens’ oath, “I will not ask who signed”. The Third Door’s sweeper, Hou Zhe, keeps a Door-book; the hinge was oiled for eleven years by someone who left no name, and a message for “Wan” has waited fifteen years.
- **Attunement** is once per element: +6 speed for a kindred root, +3 for a partial one or a deaf root that understands.

## Ages and the Age Roads

- **The register says the Wanderer is twenty.** The Wanderer was five on the Sealing Night, fifteen years before the story, and has carried the black seed since. The age the player chooses (sixteen, twenty-four, thirty-six, fifty-two or sixty-eight) is the age of the *body*. **The seed spends years where it wakes**: it has spent few of a sixteen-year-old’s and nearly all of a sixty-eight-year-old’s. The Elder says so in the Reserve (`reserve_b1`, “The Basket”). Years are spent with the work the body does, one for every ten decisions, not with the days the scenes name; each is logged as “Another year is spent in the body.”
- **A realm is a span.** Mortal 76, Qi Condensation 110, Foundation Establishment 160, Core Formation 250, Nascent Soul 400, Spirit Severing 620, Dao Seeking 950, Ascendant 1,500. A breakthrough lengthens the span and says so. A hero within three years of it is slowed ten points, past it twenty-five, and the Elder and the keepers say so. A cultivator who has risen looks younger than they are.
- **“At your age”** is a thing the Elder says only to a hero near her own years. To a hero who begins at fifty-two or sixty-eight the engine says “at your stage”.
- **The Age Roads** are eight waysides, one at each realm from Mortal to Ascendant, of five roads each, one for each age of keeper: a girl or boy of sixteen, a young adult of twenty-five, a keeper of thirty-eight, one of fifty-four and one of seventy. Mortal: the Almshouse Row (the gate). Qi Condensation: the Cold Thread Cloister. Foundation: the Level Floors. Core: the Gold Hours. Nascent Soul: the Quiet Rooms. Spirit Severing: the Unwritten Table. Dao Seeking: the Leaning House. Ascendant: the Open Sill.
- **The door of years.** On each road, in the middle of the second approach, a door opens to a hero by how far their years stand from the keeper’s: the same stretch of life, older, younger, and for the road of a youth or of an elder, far older or far younger. A hero sees one scene behind it. One of its four picks gives years to a hero who has twenty or fewer left, a realm near the road’s, and the qi to pay.
- **Seven turning points** (the gate, the Law Hall, the abyss path, the archive, the sutra door, the second descent, the sealing night) offer a hero of nineteen or less, of thirty-two to sixty-two, or of sixty-three or more a way of meeting the scene that only their years allow.

## Customs, gender and the people who carry them

- **Lian Sheng and Old Lin are women** in every book. Lian Sheng (the crib, the clerk, the festival, the summit, the archive) was a man in some lines of Books Five and Six; Old Lin (the gale, the bridge, the archive) was a man in five lines. Both are “she”.
- **Ten customs** of the sect, one wayside each: lineage (the memorial), mats (the hermit hut), ledgers (the archive index), names (the second sutra inscriptions), matches (the jade eye letter), council (the assembly hub back), mourning, hands (the ward entry), arms (the coast close) and kin (the pilgrims’ close). A keeper either holds the custom as it was handed down or bends it; none of them is a villain.
- **The Elder** is a woman of forty-three who has kept the Law twelve years. She says what it cost her to be read as one thing in a hall that expected another, and does not claim any gender has it easier. She answers a woman, a man or someone who gives no answer differently.
- **A hero is never “a girl”, “a lady”, “madam” or “she” to the whole cast.** Lines about the hero use the pronoun, honorific and person slots.
- **A hug is mercy, not truth.** The chain, the mill, the festival and the singing scenes tag the embrace as mercy.

## The ranks of the sect (fixed in the Rank Roads)

The sect says it reads a stranger’s rank, and it reads their sex as well. **The higher the rung, the less the sect admits to reading either, and the more it leans on both.** There is one rung for every realm, and the same eight places hold on every road that meets them:

| Realm | Rung | What it does with rank and gender |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mortal | the roll of hands | Three columns: water (women), loads (men), spare (everyone else, the longest and the lowest paid). A sleeve outranks every apron of either sex. |
| Qi Condensation | the novices’ halls | A white hall, a grey hall and a passage between. Novices are ranked by days since the first breath; the bath hours were set by the bath-keeper to keep a girl from bathing before a boy was awake, and the sect took them for a rule about qi. |
| Foundation Establishment | the pairing board | Pairs are drawn by realm first, sex second and fee third. A cross-pair costs a fee, which was a kindness before it was a principle. A column for ‘other’ has been empty for sixty years. |
| Core Formation | the council seats | A ring of twelve chairs and a stool: hearth chairs (women), door chairs (men), the thirteenth place for whoever gives no answer. Rank gets a chair, sex decides which, and the minute-clerk writes every word of a door chair and every third of a hearth chair. |
| Nascent Soul | the soul-portraits | The soul has no sex. The ledger paints it a robe anyway. Sixty frames hang: forty-one men, nineteen women, one in progress. |
| Spirit Severing | the severing day | Three cords: a household, a name, a debt. A woman is asked to cut the household first and a man the name; someone who gives no answer is asked what they will cut. A severed woman signs her own seal; a severed man is not asked to carry his house. |
| Dao Seeking | the companions’ table | A sun end and a moon end and a stool at the head. The seekers’ proverb: the Dao has no side, but the table has two. |
| Ascendant | the last column | The copy of the Ledger’s last page has two columns and a margin line, ‘and the rest’. No sect has had an Ascendant in two hundred years, and the copyists say it is because of the question. |

A **keeper** at a rung reads a stranger either rank first or sex first, and each way has its own kindness and its own blind spot: the keeper who reads rank first never counts a sex (so the sect cannot say how many of its sleeves are women), and the keeper who reads sex first puts people where they will be safe (and has not asked whether safe is where they wished to be). Nobody on the roads is a villain.


## The ladder: ten realms of ten layers

The ladder has **ten major realms**, each of **ten layers** (minor realms): a hundred levels, written `realm * 10 + layer`, from level 0 (Mortal, First Layer) to level 99 (Dao Sovereign, Tenth Layer). The thresholds of the realms are fixed: 0, 15, 120, 400, 850, 1,300, 1,850, 2,350, 2,950 and 3,700 points. The layers sit between them, narrow at the bottom of a realm and wide at the top, so the Tenth Layer is a long climb.

| Realm | Its ten layers | What the wall at the top of it asks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mortal | Skin, Flesh, Sinew, Bone, Marrow, Blood, Breath, Organ, Spirit-Gate, Qi-Sensing | that the qi of heaven and earth be asked to stay |
| Qi Condensation | First Thread, Cord, Skein, Braid, Rope, Rill, Stream, Pool, Tide, Brimming | that scattered qi be pressed into a floor |
| Foundation Establishment | Footing, Course, Lintel, Pillar, Beam, Floor, Wall, Roof, Hearth, Keystone | that a house be crushed into a jewel |
| Core Formation | Seed, Spark, Ember, Kernel, Gilding, Anvil, Quench, Temper, Lustre, Round | that the core break to let the person out |
| Nascent Soul | Quickening, Seed-Eye, First Breath, Cradle, Crawl, First Step, First Word, Wander, Youth, Majority | that the grown soul cut its cords |
| Spirit Severing | Names, Kin, Debts, Grudges, Vows, Faces, Home, Years, Self, Last Cord | that nothing be turned toward but the Dao |
| Dao Seeking | Hearing, Bearing, Fork, Ford, Switchback, Pass, Glimpse, Lost Road, Blind Walk, Threshold | that the seeker stop seeking |
| Ascendant | Lintel, Latch, Sill, Draught, Stair, Light, Hush, Ajar, Hinge, Doorway | that the empty doorway be stepped through |
| True Immortal | Stepping, Footfall, Weightless, Long Day, Garden, Seasons, Witness, Remembering, Lending, Lastness | that the Dao be lifted as a keeper, not a pilgrim |
| Dao Sovereign | Name, Seat, Law, Scale, Court, Mercy, Weather, Tide, Silence, Apex | none: the Apex has no wall above it |

- **Walls.** Every step up is a wall. To push at it the hero needs the points, `4 + n/2` qi and `n/3` insight (`n` the level entered). Whether it gives depends on steadiness against a difficulty of `(2(n−10))/9`, plus two for a major wall, with nothing asked of the first ten. A hero lacking qi or insight is held (once told, in the log); one not steady enough is thrown back, loses `3 + n/6` qi and half the way to the wall, and gains three points of steadiness at that wall for each fall (up to eight), so no wall keeps a hero out for ever. A layer taken without having practised against the wall costs `1 + n/12` qi; one taken after practising gives two.
- **The Elder** stopped at the top of Dao Seeking. She reached the head of the Switchback Stair twice and wanted the door so badly that it stayed a wall; she has seen the Open Door from the head of the stair, and not gone in. She cut nine cords at the Cutting Hall and kept one, and does not say which. She cracked a core at the Gold Forge long ago. She has never been to the Long Garden or the Seat of the Mountain.
- **Ascendant is no longer the top.** The sect has had no Ascendant in two hundred years, so a hero who becomes one is the first, and a True Immortal or a Dao Sovereign is something the sect has no word for. The older roads, written for the eight realms from Mortal to Ascendant, receive a True Immortal or a Sovereign as an Ascendant is received.

## Beasts, spirits and machines (fixed in the Hunt Roads)

The creatures of the roads are the sect’s old leftovers, and each is here because of something people did. None is a villain, and each has a reason (`lore` in its spec) that a hero who searches its lair learns. **A foe is never a person of the story**, and no foe is named after one (a test checks it). The facts the hunts commit to:

- **The fourteen and the fifteenth.** The ash-stag walks the dead path every dawn because the last of the fourteen, a girl of fifteen, looked back, and the path has waited for her to turn forward. The struck-out is a name from the ledger of the sealed with no person left. The sealed one under the Ash Bridge is *one of the sealed whom the Law’s ledger records as gone, who was only kneeling and listening*, which is canon for the fourteenth. The hunts say nothing of how many of the fourteen were girls, boys, or what any of them were called.
- **The bell.** The hollow bell is a bell the sect never rang, fallen into the abyss long ago. The Tribunal’s own bell is not in it.
- **The Sealing Night.** The Elder was Registrar, she signed, and Mengjiao was sealed by the masters alone. No line of the new text has the Elder standing on the lip of the cleft. The memory in the mirror shows the hero at the altar and at the cleft; the hero’s choices there are marked (`st_<tone>_<scene>`) and eight are named (`sn_*`).
- **The Glass Sea has no water.** The wreck fleet’s mirage is a reflection that has lost its lake; its scorpion hunts by the thrum of the glass.
- **The Wanderer is a foundling.** A voice-fox that calls in “a mother’s voice” calls in the one the hero would have chosen, never one they had.
- **Gender and the hunt.** Every hub’s hunters have a rule about who hunts and who guards the door. The rule is old, and kept from habit. A woman, a man and someone who gives no answer each have a stroke of their own in every fight, and a sentence of their own from every foe.

### The edge

A fight reads the hero against the foe (`src/engine/fight.ts`): a point for every three levels above the foe’s level (below, against; at most four either way), the hero’s rank in their own Dao (0 to 4, none without a Dao), how their roots answer the element it fears (0, 1 or 2), two if the foe’s bane is their Dao, one for qi of 60 or more and minus one for 10 or less, and a foothold (up to three) for each of the three turns of the fight at which the hero chose in the foe’s soft tone. The range is −8 to 14. **A stroke by strength is open from +3, a craft from +1 (with a Dao), a ruse from −1 (with Insight 20); below +3 the hero may press on and be thrown down, and a hero may always break off.** A plain hero three layers up the realm can end every one of the forty by strength if they read the foe, and by guile if they did not; a test checks both.
